# Idea generation

Contents: starting points, tips when stuck, core dynamics list, unblocking moves, turning an idea into a playable frame.

## Starting points

### Start from an existing game

Every game builds on what came before. Use a game as a jumping-off point and explore the design space that the change opens. A reskin is not the goal.

| Move | Example |
|---|---|
| Change the perspective | A top-down factory game moved into first person |
| Change the theme or context | A block survival game moved underwater |
| Change the medium | A parlor social-deduction game made digital |
| Revive a neglected formula | A farming sim made because the old series declined |
| Filter it through your own taste | A found prototype reborn with a new style, music and tone |

### Start from a genre

A genre is a recipe of ready-made mechanics and conventions. Bend it.

- Find the genre's recurring problem and fix it. A dungeon crawler about skill instead of dice luck. A city builder with beauty and no creeping complexity.
- Combine two genres so each repairs the other's weakness. Platforming without repeated levels plus roguelike variety without unfair randomness.
- Add something new, or take something away. A platformer hero who cannot jump becomes a slower, methodical game.
- Keep the mechanics, swap the metaphor. Lining up a crosshair can be a camera or a pressure washer instead of a gun.
- Treat genres loosely. Boil the genre down to its DNA and build outward. Do not fixate on what a game needs to "count".

### Start from a new mechanic (bottom up)

- Real-world activities and jobs: checking documents at a border, gardening, running a shop. Having interests outside games matters.
- The input device: a swipe that feels like a slice, an analog trigger that feels like a water pistol.
- One isolated mechanic from another game, expanded into a whole game: a rewind power, a downward thrust, a magnetic glove.

Then find a theme and aesthetic that make the mechanic feel natural.

### Start from an experience (top down)

- A fantasy or role: commander of a failing spaceship, ruler of a tiny kingdom.
- A theme: death treated gently, collateral damage in monster fights.
- A personal experience.
- An image, a piece of music, a mood.

Then choose mechanics that deliver that experience.

## Tips when stuck

1. Break games down into their smallest elements. Keep a mental periodic table of mechanics and systems to remix.
2. Interrogate what you play like a critic. Flaws are opportunities.
3. Challenge conventions. Ask what happens if an assumed rule is flipped (a board game that remembers previous sessions).
4. Play everything, across genres and media.
5. Impose a limitation. A blank page is harder than a box.
6. Enter game jams. Themes are both a limit and a spark ("limited space" became a racing game inside a parking garage).
7. Open the editor and noodle. Some ideas only appear while making things.
8. Surround yourself with music and art.
9. Do something else. Ideas arrive when the mind wanders, not in forced brainstorm meetings.
10. Keep the idea small. It is a seed to grow through development, and you may not make it at all.

## Core dynamics to build around

A core is the single thing the gameplay is about. These patterns recur across digital and tabletop games. Pick one as the spine and tie every feature back to it.

| Core dynamic | What the player is doing |
|---|---|
| Territorial acquisition | Taking and holding a limited space |
| Prediction | Guessing what will happen, weighing odds |
| Spatial reasoning | Fitting, arranging, planning placement |
| Survival | Staying alive as the main activity, not just a lose condition |
| Destruction | Wrecking everything in sight |
| Building | Growing a character, city or engine |
| Collection | Matching and gathering sets |
| Chasing or evading | Catching or escaping |
| Trading | Negotiating and exchanging resources |
| Race to the end | Getting somewhere or finishing something first |

A feature set that works is part innovation, part improvement, part convention.

## Unblocking moves for a design that is stuck

Each move forces a large change in dynamics. Apply one, play a session, observe.

- Make a resource limited, or unlimited. List every resource, flip one, replay.
- Let players interfere with or help each other. Ask how someone could stop this action or speed it up, and how the target could protect themselves.
- Mess with the play order: interrupts, extra turns, skipped turns.
- Kill a rule. Rules pile up when designers do not trust players. Remove every rule that does not serve the core.
- Use the rule of two: double or halve one value. A drastic change shows how values interact far better than a small tweak.

## From idea to playable frame

Before judging any idea, write the playable frame from SKILL.md for a single level, match or session: goal, obstacle, fail state, actions.

Example: a taxi game. Goal: earn a target fare total by delivering passengers. Obstacle: a ticking timer plus traffic and scenery. Fail state: time runs out. Actions: drive fast and recklessly.

Then answer the player's questions: What is this game about? How do I play? How do I win? Why do I want to play? What things do I need to do?
